Best Trays for Growing Microgreens at Home

Apartment tray picker: DIY containers vs 1010 vs 1020, why you need holes + a solid drip tray, shallow vs deep, and the specs that prevent warps and mold.

On this page The two-tray system DIY, 1010, or 1020 Shallow vs deep Buy checklist DIY conversion Wash and reuse

“Best tray” is the wrong first question. The right question is whether you have a perforated grow tray nested in a solid watering tray. That pair enables bottom watering, catches drips before they stain a rental counter, and is what university extension guides quietly assume when they say “water from the bottom.” Brand comes second.

This page helps apartment growers pick a size that fits a counter or shelf, without jumping straight into commercial 10-packs. Pair tray choice with bottom watering and mold prevention.

The only setup that consistently works

  • Grow tray with drainage holes — holds medium and seed; excess water must escape.
  • Solid drip / flood tray — same footprint, no holes; holds water temporarily and catches leaks.
  • Optional third tray or board — blackout cover for the first 1–3 days; sometimes lightly weighted for large seed.

Maryland Extension describes commercial microgreen sets as a drained tray sitting inside an undrained reservoir and warns that careful watering is still required so the mix does not saturate. Utah State notes that 1020 trays work well and that pie tins or takeout trays are fine starters if they drain.

Apartment add-on: put the nested pair on a baking sheet or boot tray. A warped cheap flat full of wet coir can dump more water than you expect.

Bootstrap Farmer–style guidance (and similar home blogs) maps cleanly to apartments: choose 1010 trays for small households that want variety; choose a few 1020 trays if you eat greens often or grow thirsty large-seed crops. Two 1010 grow trays can sometimes share watering logistics with one larger solid tray — only if water still spreads evenly and you still dump leftovers.

Suggested buy order: (1) DIY one crop, (2) one quality 1010 holes + 1010 solid pair, (3) add a 1020 pair only after you finish two clean harvests.

Shallow vs deep trays

  • Shallow ~1–1¼ inch (≈2.5–3.2 cm) — enough for most brassicas and herbs; less medium cost; easier to cut stems low.
  • Deep ~2.5 inch (≈6.4 cm) — useful for pea shoots, sunflower, wheatgrass, or arid rooms that dry shallow trays too fast.

Match depths when nesting. A shallow grow tray in a much deeper solid tray can sit oddly and water unevenly. Medium depth inside the grow tray is still only about 1.5–2.5 cm for most microgreens — you are not filling a deep tray to the brim with soil.

Buy checklist (before you click “add to cart”)

  1. Food-safe plastic — look for reusable PP (#5) or clearly labeled food-grade trays. Avoid mystery brittle flats that crack on week two.
  2. Hole count / distribution — many purpose-built shallow 1020s use on the order of ~30–40 drainage holes spread across the base so wick-up is even.
  3. Wall thickness — heavy-duty trays (~1.8–2.0 mm in some USA-made lines) resist bowing when full of wet coir.
  4. True nest fit — grow tray must sit level in the solid tray with room for a thin water layer (~5–12 mm / ¼–½ inch when watering).
  5. Dishwasher or sink-friendly — you will wash every cycle; texture that traps biofilm in corners is a mold factory.
  6. Standard footprint — 1010/1020 sizes fit common shelves, humidity domes, and seed charts.

Flimsy big-box seed trays can work once or twice. They are the usual source of mid-grow warps and corner leaks. If your “tray budget” is tiny, spend it on one sturdy pair rather than five disposable flats.

DIY conversion that is actually safe on a counter

  1. Pick a shallow food container (clear salad clamshells and pie tins are common).
  2. Drill or melt a grid of 3–5 mm holes every few centimeters across the base — not only the center.
  3. Smooth sharp plastic edges so they do not scratch sinks or hands.
  4. Set inside a solid plate, lid, or second container with no holes.
  5. Test empty with 100 ml of water on a towel: confirm even spread and no side-seam leaks.
  6. Place the stack on a waterproof mat before adding medium.

DIY is perfect for learning sowing density. Upgrade when you hate washing awkward shapes or when seed charts in grams-per-1020 become annoying to rescale.

Wash, dry, repeat

After harvest, empty medium, scrub with hot soapy water, and optionally follow with a diluted vinegar rinse or a food-safe peroxide wipe (never mixed together). Air-dry fully before nesting for storage. Wet stacked trays grow mold before your next sow even starts.

How many pairs do you need? For personal use: one germinating, one under lights, one clean spare is a comfortable rhythm — three pairs. That is enough to keep a continuous harvest without covering the entire kitchen.

Once trays and watering are dialed, crop choice and light become the fun parts. Start with forgiving varieties from our beginner list, and keep the first rack simple.
