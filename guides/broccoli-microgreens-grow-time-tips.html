Broccoli Microgreens: Grow Time and Practical Tips

Broccoli is a mild brassica that fills a tray fast, tastes like young broccoli stems rather than hot mustard, and usually hits harvest in about a week to ten days. This page is the apartment version: seed amounts, soak or not, blackout length, light height, bottom watering, and the mold traps that catch beginners.

If you have never run a tray, skim the small-apartment starter workflow first. Broccoli sits next to radish and peas on most beginner shortlists — see our easiest microgreens for beginners for how it compares.

Why broccoli is a strong apartment crop

Broccoli microgreens are Brassica oleracea harvested as seedlings. They earn a counter spot for three practical reasons:

  • Fast: most trays are ready in 7–10 days from sow (some land closer to 8–12 depending on room temperature and light).
  • Mild flavor: fresh broccoli stem with a light pepper finish — friendlier than radish or mustard if you cook for picky eaters.
  • Dense, cuttable canopy: small seed packs evenly into a shallow tray and stands up to scissors without the tall flop of poorly lit peas.

They also skip the long overnight soak that sunflower and peas need, which cuts one mess step in a small kitchen. The trade-off: brassicas mold easily if you sow too thick or keep the leaf tops wet. Density and airflow matter more here than fancy gear.

Grow timeline day by day

Times assume roughly 18–24 °C (65–75 °F) and a simple LED a few inches above the canopy. Cooler rooms run slower; warm, still kitchens run faster and mold risk rises.

Rule of thumb: blackout about 2–3 days (up to 4 if cool), then 4–7 days under light. Cut before true leaves dominate — that is when stems start to toughen and flavor drifts toward cabbage.

Seed rate and tray size

Broccoli seed is tiny. Weighing beats tablespoons when you can, because seed lots vary. Practical apartment rates:

  • 10×20-inch (1020) tray: about 18–25 g dry seed. ~20 g is a solid starter rate for a full canopy without a mold carpet. Heavier rates near 28 g work but demand excellent airflow.
  • 10×10-inch (1010) / half tray: about 9–12 g (roughly half a 1020).
  • Tablespoon fallback: roughly 2–2½ tablespoons for a 1020, 1–1¼ tablespoons for a 1010 — verify once with a kitchen scale if you have one.

Buy seed labeled for sprouting or microgreens, not treated garden seed. Spread so seeds sit close but mostly in a single layer — gaps and thick piles both cause uneven trays.

Sowing, medium, and blackout vs dome

Medium

Use about 2–3 cm (¾–1 in) of pre-moistened coconut coir or a fine seed-starting mix. Level and press lightly flat. Coir holds water evenly with less mess; seed-start mix is fine if it drains. Fresh medium every crop — do not reuse spent trays.

Sow dry (no long soak)

  1. Scatter seed evenly. Break apart clumps with a fingertip.
  2. Press gently so every seed touches the medium. Do not bury under a soil layer.
  3. Mist until the seed layer looks evenly damp — that settles the seed. A short rinse is optional; a multi-hour soak is unnecessary and often adds surface moisture you do not want.

Blackout

Cover with an upside-down tray or opaque lid for ~2–3 days. A light weight (another tray, or a small book on a full 1020) helps roots push down; broccoli does not need the heavy brick sunflower likes. Check daily. Mist only if the surface looks dry — do not flood.

When stems are pale and lifting the cover, remove the weight. You can leave a raised dome or inverted tray for one more short dark stretch if the mat is uneven, then move to light. A clear humidity dome left on too long after greening is a mold trap for brassicas — prefer a short blackout, then open air.

Nested trays (drainage tray inside a solid catch tray) make later bottom watering easy; see trays for home microgreens if you are still shopping.

Light, watering, and humidity notes for brassicas

  • Light: LED shop light or grow light about 10–20 cm (4–8 in) above the canopy, 12–16 hours a day. Raise the fixture as the greens grow so leaves do not cook. A bright south or west window can work; expect taller, leaner stems and rotate the tray daily.
  • Water: once uncovered, stop misting the leaves. Pour water into the solid outer tray, let the medium soak 10–20 minutes, then dump leftover water. Details in the bottom watering guide. Lift the tray each morning — water when it feels light.
  • Airflow: a small clip fan on low, aimed past (not blasting) the canopy, dries leaf surfaces between waterings. Brassicas hate a wet, still canopy more than they hate mild drafts.
  • Mold risk: too thick a sow + wet tops + no air is the classic failure. Thin the next sow slightly and keep tops dry. Pair this page with apartment mold prevention.
Fine white fuzz on roots: short root hairs are normal. Gray cobweb mycelium climbing stems with a musty smell is mold — improve airflow, bottom water only, and sow thinner next time.

Harvest cues and storage

Cut when cotyledons (seed leaves) are fully open and green, usually at 5–8 cm (2–3 in). The first true leaves may just be showing as tiny points — that is the sweet spot. Waiting until true leaves are large makes stems stringier and the flavor more cabbage-like.

  • Use clean scissors or a sharp knife. Cut just above the medium so you leave grit behind.
  • Harvest dry — wet leaves spoil faster in the fridge.
  • One cut per tray. Broccoli microgreens do not give a useful second harvest like pea shoots.
  • Store in a lidded container lined with paper towel in the fridge. Expect about 5–7 days of good quality at home. Full method: harvest and store microgreens.

Short checklist

  1. Fill tray with 2–3 cm damp coir or seed-start mix; nest in a solid catch tray.
  2. Sow ~20 g dry broccoli seed per 1020 (~10 g per 1010); press and mist.
  3. Blackout 2–3 days with light cover/weight; mist only if surface dries.
  4. Uncover; LED 10–20 cm above canopy, 12–16 h/day.
  5. Bottom water; dump leftover water; keep a gentle fan nearby.
  6. Harvest day 7–10 at open cotyledons, cut above medium; fridge in a paper-towel lined box.

Bottom line: broccoli microgreens are a no-soak, week-scale brassica. Keep the sow even and slightly conservative, blackout short, lights close, and water from below — that is most of the difference between a clean green mat and a moldy carpet.
