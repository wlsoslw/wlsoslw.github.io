Pea Shoots Indoors: A Practical Grow Guide

Pea shoots are the most forgiving big-seed crop you can grow on a counter. They like cool rooms, shrug off a little neglect, taste like fresh garden peas, and can often give you a second cut from the same tray.

Pea shoots grow differently from tiny-seed microgreens like broccoli or radish. Each pea is a big, energy-packed seed, so the plant can grow tall, tendrilled and leafy without much help. That makes them great for apartments: they tolerate a cooler room than most crops, they do not need a perfect light setup, and they are hard to kill. The main ways to fail are buying the wrong seed, sowing too thickly, and keeping the tray soggy.

If you have never grown a tray before, skim the apartment starter workflow first. Pea shoots are a strong first or second crop alongside the others in our easiest microgreens list.

Which peas to buy (and avoid)

  • Buy peas sold for sprouting, shoots, or microgreens. Common choices are speckled peas, dun peas, and green field peas (sometimes labeled “pea shoots” or “Dundale”). They are cheap, sprout reliably, and grow sturdy shoots.
  • Snow and sugar snap pea seed also grow nice tender shoots, but garden packets are pricey per tray. Use them if you already have them.
  • Avoid treated garden seed. Some garden pea seed is coated with fungicide (often brightly colored pink, blue or green). It is not meant to be eaten as a shoot. Check the packet.
  • Never use ornamental sweet peas. Sweet pea flowers (Lathyrus odoratus) are a different plant and are not edible. If the packet is in the flower section, it is the wrong seed.
  • Dried split peas from the grocery store will not sprout — they have been split and the embryo is damaged. Whole dried peas sometimes sprout, but germination is a gamble.

Rough amount: about 200–280 g (7–10 oz) of dry seed per 10×20-inch tray. For a half-size tray, start near 110–140 g. Peas look like a lot of seed for one tray; that is normal.

Soaking

  1. Rinse the dry peas and pick out broken or shriveled ones.
  2. Cover with cool water by at least twice their depth. Peas roughly double in size as they drink.
  3. Soak 8–12 hours, usually overnight. In a warm room, change the water halfway or keep the soak toward 8 hours.
  4. Drain and rinse. You can sow right away, or let them sit in a strainer for another 8–12 hours, rinsing once or twice, until small root tips appear. The pre-sprout gives a more even tray.
Smell check: soaked peas should smell fresh and slightly beany. A sour, fermented smell means the soak ran too long or too warm. Rinse well; if the smell does not go away, start over.

Sowing

Fill your tray with about 2–3 cm (1 inch) of pre-moistened seed-starting mix or coir — see coir vs soil for the trade-offs. Level it and press it lightly flat.

  • Spread the soaked peas in a single layer, seeds touching but not stacked. A double layer of wet peas is the classic recipe for rot in the middle of the tray.
  • Press them gently into the surface. No need to cover them with medium.
  • Mist the seed layer once so it is evenly damp.

A tray with drainage holes nested inside a solid tray works best for this crop because it makes bottom watering easy later. Our tray guide covers the options, including cheap DIY containers.

A short blackout

Peas do not need as long a blackout as sunflower, but a few days of covered darkness with some weight still helps the roots anchor and the tray come up evenly.

  1. Cover the tray with an upside-down tray or lid.
  2. Add light weight: about 1–3 kg (2–6 lb) on a full tray is plenty.
  3. Keep it covered for 2–4 days. Check daily and mist only if the surface looks dry.
  4. Uncover when the shoots are about 3–5 cm tall and pushing the cover up.

Fine white hairs on pea roots are normal root hairs. Gray, cobweb-like fuzz spreading over the seeds with a musty smell is mold — see mold prevention.

Light and temperature

  • Peas like it cool. Roughly 15–21 °C (60–70 °F) suits them well, which makes them a good crop for a cool apartment in autumn and winter. Above about 24 °C (75 °F), they grow fast but floppy and mold risk goes up.
  • Light: a simple LED shop light or grow light, a few inches to about a foot above the shoots, on 12–16 hours a day. Our cheap grow light setup shows a low-cost build.
  • Windowsill-only works better with peas than with most crops because the big seed carries the plant. Expect taller, paler, leaning shoots; rotate the tray daily.
  • Expect about 7–10 more days under light before the first cut.

Watering

Once the shoots are up, stop misting from above. Water from below: pour water into the solid outer tray, let the medium soak for 10–20 minutes, then pour off the rest. Details are in our bottom watering guide.

Pea trays drink a lot in the last few days because there is so much leaf. Lift the tray each morning: a light tray needs water, a heavy one does not. Keep a small fan on low nearby so leaves and the soil surface dry between waterings.

Harvest and second cut

Most growers cut pea shoots at about 8–15 cm (3–6 inches), usually 10–14 days after soaking. Shorter shoots are more tender; taller shoots with tendrils look prettier on a plate but the lower stem gets stringy.

  • Cut with clean scissors above the lowest leaf node (a little higher than you would cut other microgreens) if you want a second harvest. The plant can regrow side shoots from the nodes below the cut.
  • A second cut is usually smaller and a bit tougher, ready about 7–10 days later. Most growers stop after two cuts and compost the tray.
  • Taste a shoot from the bottom of the stem. If it is fibrous, cut higher next time or harvest a few days earlier.

Harvest dry, then store in a lidded container lined with paper towel in the fridge, as described in our harvest and storage guide. Pea shoots typically keep about 5–7 days.

Bottom line: buy untreated peas meant for sprouting, soak overnight, sow one even layer, give them two to four covered days, then grow them cool under decent light with bottom watering. Cut above a node at a hand’s height and you may get a bonus second harvest from the same tray.
